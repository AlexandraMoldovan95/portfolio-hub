Job Tracker

Track the companies you apply to, where each application stands, and your resume versions. Each account sees only its own data, and I tested that from the outside: a second account couldn't read, change or delete anything in mine. Try it without an account: the "Login demo" button opens it with sample data.

What it does

Companies, applications with their status, a dashboard (response rate, top companies), resume versions you can export, and deleting your account with everything in it.

My role

Product idea, UX, the privacy rules (which account may read or change which rows) and the testing below. The code was written with an AI assistant (Claude).

Proof

10 of 10 data-isolation checks · 7 + 13 delete-account checks · 19 unit tests on every push.

Status

Live · MIT · source on GitHub.

How I tested it

The biggest risk in an app with accounts is one person seeing someone else's data. Hiding it in the interface isn't enough, so the rules live in the database (Supabase Row Level Security). I tested them from outside the app: direct database calls from a second, throwaway account (B), against a company I had created with my own account (A).

Data isolation: 10 of 10 checks passed

In plain words: account B tried to read, change and delete my data and was refused every time, while it could still save and delete its own.

Run on the live project on 24 November 2026. The control row matters: it shows the refusals come from who owns the row, not from a setup that refuses everything. Deleting an account was tested the same way, with 7 checks through the API and 13 in the browser, all passed, including that the deleted account can't log in again and that its rows are really gone, not just hidden. What testing and reviews found

  • A gap in the database rules, found in an AI code review I asked for. An application could be attached to another user's company through a direct API call. The rule now also checks that the company is yours (25 November 2026). Checked by reading the rule; not yet re-tested with two accounts on the live project.
  • A bug found while writing the unit tests. A resume named with spaces only was exported as ".html", with no name. Fixed.
  • A form that could lose your typing. A click outside a form closed it, so selecting text with the mouse could throw away what you had typed. Clicking outside no longer closes it, and unsaved changes are marked.

Design case study: a neon theme that still reads like a tool

A job tracker usually looks generic. I wanted this one to be memorable, but it's a tool you open every day to read your own data, so the look can't get in the way of reading.

Start from a style, then tone it down

I started from a vaporwave/outrun style (near-black purple, neon cyan and magenta, slanted buttons, CRT scanlines) and toned it down for real data: subtler scanlines, the button slant cut from 12° to 6°, status badges as neon outlines instead of filled pills, and error messages on black instead of light grey on neon red. The exported resume stays plain, because it goes to real employers. Later came a daytime version of the same look, with every text meeting WCAG AA contrast in both themes.

Design iteration (November 2026)

A design review pointed at the login screen: a visitor without an account had to find "Login demo" under the real login. The demo is now the first, filled button. The inputs moved from pure black boxes onto the card's surface, and placeholder text went from 1.97:1 to 6.56:1 contrast.

How it's built

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with no framework or build step, talking directly to Supabase (a hosted Postgres database with login).

  • The key in the page is public by design. Supabase's publishable key is meant to be seen; what protects the data is the database rules tested above.
  • A payment flow shown, not faked. "Support the project" opens a real Stripe checkout in test mode: no live key anywhere, so no real charge can happen.
  • Security headers from the server. A Content-Security-Policy sent by Netlify, with no inline scripts or styles allowed, against injected scripts and clickjacking.
  • Installable, without a stale copy. It installs like an app and shows an offline screen, but doesn't keep a cached copy of the app, so nobody gets stuck on an old version after an update.
